# AVX-512 register tiles (`gemm_simd_blocked<Zmm>`)

`gemm_simd_blocked<Zmm, R, V, …>` multiplies with an R × V register tile:
R rows of C, V vectors of 16 FP32 / 8 FP64 each, kept in R·V ZMM
accumulators for a whole k block. The default is **8 × 3** (8 × 48 FP32,
8 × 24 FP64). This page gives the measurements behind that choice on AMD
Zen 5. Code: [`avx512.hpp`](../../src/gemm/avx512.hpp)
(`vector_micro<Zmm<T>, R, V>`), driver
[`simd.hpp`](../../src/gemm/simd.hpp). The other
families: [AVX2](avx2.md), [NEON](neon.md).

## What a tile shape decides

The same three numbers as for [AVX2](avx2.md#what-a-tile-shape-decides),
with Zen 5's AVX-512 figures:

- **Accumulators, R·V:** 2 FMAs per cycle × 4 cycles latency, so 8 at
  least and 12 for the full rate. Peak, measured with `fma_peak`:
  362 GFLOP/s FP32, 180 FP64.
- **Registers, R·V + V (+ 1):** 32 ZMM. GCC either keeps the broadcast of
  A in a register or folds it into the FMA as an embedded broadcast
  (`{1to16}`).
- **B traffic, 64 / R bytes per FMA.**

And one more that only the machine code shows: **whether B is loaded into
registers at all.** Counted in the disassembly of each shape (GCC 13,
`-march=native`):

| Shapes | How the k loop reads B and A |
|---|---|
| 4 × 2, 2 × 4, 4 × 4 | every FMA takes its B vector from memory: V·R loads of B per k step instead of V |
| 6 × 2, 8 × 2, 6 × 3, 8 × 3, 12 × 2, 14 × 2 | B loaded once per k step into registers, every FMA register-only |
| 16 × 1 | one B load, and all 16 broadcasts of A folded into the FMAs: 17 loads for 16 FMAs |

Zen 5 performs at most two 512-bit loads per cycle, so a k step of R·V
FMAs (R·V / 2 cycles) is load-bound whenever it needs more than R·V loads.
That is the case for the first and the last row of the table. Part 1 tells
how this was found:
[a code-generation trap](../articles/simd-gemm.md#why-the-tile-has-8-rows-a-code-generation-trap).

The sweep ran with the cache block of the time, 64 × 256 × 512 (TileM × TileK
× TileN); the blocks were swept afterwards with the chosen tile
([blocks.md](blocks.md)). The tiles were not re-swept with the new
blocks.

¹ FP64 N = 512 puts B's rows 4 KiB apart, where the result depends on
where the allocator places the three matrices. Run alone (`Avx512Blocked`
or `Avx512Tile8x3` filtered by itself), 8 × 3 measures 126–128 here;
8 × 2 stays at 101–103 either way.

On L1-resident data (`simd_microkernel`, FP32, B packed) the shapes that
load B into registers all run at 95–97% of peak: 6, 8 and 12 rows × 2
vectors reach 345.5, 349.5 and 349.1 GFLOP/s, and 4 × 2 reaches 142.5.
The differences in the sweep therefore come from the cache, not from the
FMA pipeline.

## Why 8 × 3

- **At least 6 rows, so B stays in registers.** Every shape with ≤ 4 rows
  re-loads B in every FMA and is 20–68% behind the best shape at
  N ≤ 512, whatever its accumulator count (4 × 4 has 16). 16 × 1 loads
  each broadcast inside its FMA instead: 7–11% behind at N ≤ 512 FP32,
  42% at N = 1024.
- **More rows where B comes from L2.** At N = 1024 FP32 and N = 512 FP64
  (4 KiB row stride: B's rows fold onto 12 lines of L1, see
  [Part 3](../articles/sme2-cache-associativity.md#the-same-experiment-on-amd-zen-5))
  the 12-accumulator 6 × 2 loses 22–23% against 8 × 2. On L1-resident
  data with B rows 4 KiB apart the same order shows: 6, 8 and 12 rows
  reach 210.3, 274.6 and 342.5 GFLOP/s FP32, as each row added reuses each
  B load once more.
- **A third vector.** 8 × 3 has the same B bytes per FMA as 8 × 2 but
  issues 24 FMAs per 11 loads instead of 16 per 10, and it is the best or
  within 2% of the best at every size from 256 up: against 8 × 2, +10%
  FP32 at N = 1024, +16% at 4096, and FP64 about +25% at N = 512 (from
  the isolated runs of note ¹) and +26–38% at N ≥ 1024. The
  sweep does not show why the third vector helps this much where B
  misses L1; this machine allows no hardware counters to measure it.
- **Rows that divide the block.** 12 × 2 and 14 × 2 read less B per FMA,
  but the 64-row block splits into 5 × 12 + 4 or 4 × 14 + 8 rows, and
  12 × 2's 4-row remainder runs the B-from-memory 4 × 2. 14 × 2 beats
  8 × 3 at N ≤ 256 FP32 by 2–7%; from N = 1024 up 8 × 3 is ahead by
  5–15%.
- **Registers:** 8 × 3 uses 28 of the 32 ZMM registers, no spills. No
  shape in the sweep spills (14 × 2 uses 31).

`gemm_simd_blocked<Zmm>` was 4 × 2 until 2026-10-15, then 8 × 2 (Part 1's
section on the trap), and is 8 × 3 since 2026-10-21. The re-measured
kernel ([bench_zen5_avx512_8x3.csv](../experiments/tile-sweeps/bench_zen5_avx512_8x3.csv)):
325.5 GFLOP/s FP32 at N = 256, 329.0 at 512 (91% of peak), 312.2 at 1024;
FP64 162.1 at N = 256 (90%). With the cache blocks chosen since
([blocks.md](blocks.md)): 327.9 FP32 at N = 512 and 294.7 at 4096 (was
230.2); FP64 157.7 at N = 512 and 145.0 at 4096 (was 128.4 and 95.9).

## Reproduce

```bash
cmake -B build -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release -DHPC_ENABLE_AVX512=ON
cmake --build build --target bench_gemm simd_microkernel
taskset -c 2 ./build/benchmarks/bench_gemm --benchmark_filter='^Avx512Tile' \
    --benchmark_repetitions=3 --benchmark_report_aggregates_only=true
taskset -c 2 ./build/benchmarks/simd_microkernel
```

A shape not in the sweep: `gemm_simd_blocked<Zmm, R, V, TileM, TileK, TileN>(A, B, C)`, or
a line `HPC_AVX512_TILE(R, V);` in `add_tiles()` of
[`bench_gemm.cpp`](../../benchmarks/bench_gemm.cpp).
