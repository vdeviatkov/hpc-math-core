# AVX-512 register tiles (`gemm_simd_blocked<Zmm>`)

`gemm_simd_blocked<Zmm, R, V, …>` multiplies with an R × V register tile:
R rows of C, V vectors of 16 FP32 / 8 FP64 each, kept in R·V ZMM
accumulators for a whole k block. The default is **8 × 3**, which covers an
8 × 48 block of C in FP32 and 8 × 24 in FP64. This page gives the measurements behind that choice on AMD
Zen 5. Code: [`avx512.hpp`](../../src/gemm/avx512.hpp)
(`vector_micro<Zmm<T>, R, V>`), driver
[`simd.hpp`](../../src/gemm/simd.hpp). The other
families: [AVX2](avx2.md), [NEON](neon.md).

## What a tile shape decides

The same three numbers as for [AVX2](avx2.md#what-a-tile-shape-decides),
with Zen 5's AVX-512 figures:

- **Accumulators, R·V:** 2 FMAs per cycle × 4 cycles latency, so 8 at
  least and 12 for the full rate. Peak, measured with `fma_peak`:
  362 GFLOP/s FP32, 180 FP64.
- **Registers, R·V + V (+ 1):** 32 ZMM. GCC either keeps the broadcast of
  A in a register or folds it into the FMA as an embedded broadcast
  (`{1to16}`).
- **B traffic, 64 / R bytes per FMA.**

And one more that only the machine code shows: **whether B is loaded into
registers at all.** Counted in the disassembly of each shape (GCC 13,
`-march=native`), and confirmed in the running loop with `perf annotate`
(4 × 2: the hottest FMAs are `vfmadd231ps (%rdx)` and `0x40(%rdx)`):

| Shapes | How the k loop reads B and A |
|---|---|
| 4 × 2, 2 × 4, 4 × 4 | every FMA takes its B vector from memory: V·R loads of B per k step instead of V |
| 6 × 2, 8 × 2, 6 × 3, 8 × 3, 12 × 2, 14 × 2 | B loaded once per k step into registers, every FMA register-only |
| 16 × 1 | one B load and 16 broadcasts of A, each used by a single FMA: 17 loads for 16 FMAs (the broadcasts are folded into the FMAs as `{1to16}`, which does not change the count) |

Zen 5 performs at most two 512-bit loads per cycle, so a k step of R·V
FMAs (R·V / 2 cycles) is load-bound whenever it needs more than R·V loads.
That is the case for the first and the last row of the table. The limit
is measured (`x86_load_ports`): 2.0 vector loads per cycle at 128, 256 and
512 bits alike, and 1.92 loads with 1.92 FMAs per cycle when mixed
([zen5-counters](../experiments/zen5-counters/load_ports.csv)).

The folding is GCC's choice, not the shape's: Clang 18 compiles every
shape, 4 × 2 included, with B loaded once per k step and register-only
FMAs. With Clang, 4 × 2 runs at 287.0 GFLOP/s FP32 at N = 256 instead of
219.4 (+31%) and 4 × 4 at 303.2 instead of 254.7 (+19%), while 6 rows and
more run the same with both compilers (8 × 3: 328.4 against 325.9)
([data](../experiments/zen5-counters/fold/README.txt)). Part 1 tells how
the trap was found:
[a code-generation trap](../articles/simd-gemm.md#why-the-tile-has-8-rows-a-code-generation-trap).

## Tile sweep, Zen 5

`bench_gemm`'s `Avx512Tile<R>x<V>` benchmarks: the library kernel with
each shape, GFLOP/s, median of 3, pinned to one core
([data](../experiments/tile-sweeps/README.md)). Best per column in bold.
**B from memory**: the compiler never loads B into a register, and every
FMA reads its B vector from the cache as a memory operand
(`vfmadd231ps (%rdx), …`), so B is loaded R times per k step instead of
once (table above). **One broadcast per FMA**: with a single vector, each
broadcast of A feeds only one FMA, so 16 × 1 needs 17 loads per 16 FMAs
whatever the compiler does (table above).

The sweep runs each shape at the default cache blocks (512 × 256 × 512
FP32, 512 × 256 × 256 FP64, TileM × TileK × TileN; [blocks.md](blocks.md)).

On L1-resident data (`simd_microkernel`, FP32, B packed) the shapes that
load B into registers all run at 95–97% of peak: 6, 8 and 12 rows × 2
vectors reach 345.5, 349.5 and 349.1 GFLOP/s, and 4 × 2 reaches 142.5.
The differences in the sweep therefore come from the cache, not from the
FMA pipeline.

## Why 8 × 3

- **At least 6 rows, so B stays in registers.** The source loads B once
  per k step for every shape, but GCC 13 compiles every shape with ≤ 4
  rows to read B inside each FMA instead (B from memory, above); Clang 18
  does not. With GCC those shapes are 20–65% behind the best shape at
  N ≤ 512, whatever their accumulator count (4 × 4 has 16). Without the
  folding (Clang) 4 × 2 and 4 × 4 are still 8–13% behind at N = 256, and
  2 × 4, which reads 32 bytes of B per FMA, stays 48% behind. 16 × 1 keeps B in a register, but
  with one vector each broadcast of A serves a single FMA, so it needs
  more loads than FMAs: 6–11% behind at N ≤ 512 FP32, 43% at N = 1024.
- **More rows where B comes from L2.** At N = 1024 FP32 and N = 512 FP64
  a row of B is exactly 4 KiB. Counted with `perf` on the same kind of
  kernel, that stride raises L2 demand misses 1.6–1.7× against rows padded
  by 64 B, at N = 1024, 2048 and 4096; from N = 2048 up it also raises L1
  misses by 9–14% ([zen5-counters](../experiments/zen5-counters/b_stride.txt),
  [Part 3](../articles/sme2-cache-associativity.md#the-same-experiment-on-amd-zen-5)).
  There the 12-accumulator 6 × 2 loses 21–22% against 8 × 2. On L1-resident
  data with B rows 4 KiB apart the same order shows: 6, 8 and 12 rows
  reach 210.3, 274.6 and 342.5 GFLOP/s FP32, as each row added reuses each
  B load once more.
- **A third vector.** 8 × 3 has the same B bytes per FMA as 8 × 2 but
  issues 24 FMAs per 11 loads instead of 16 per 10, and it is the best or
  within 2% of the best at every size from 256 up: against 8 × 2, +12%
  FP32 at N = 1024, +29% at 4096, and FP64 +13% at N = 512 and +24–31% at
  N ≥ 1024. Counted with
  `perf` per 1,000 FP operations, the third vector does not reduce L1
  misses where it helps most (FP32 N = 1024: 4.48 against 4.51 L1 misses
  to L2) but cuts L1 TLB misses by 35–39% and load-queue stall cycles by
  55–69% at every size measured
  ([data](../experiments/zen5-counters/third_vector.txt)). Each call walks
  256 rows of B, each in its own 4 KiB page; 8 × 3 does 1.5× the FMAs per
  page, which predicts 0.67× the TLB misses; measured 0.61–0.65×.
- **Not more rows.** 12 × 2 and 14 × 2 read less B per FMA than 8 × 3.
  14 × 2 leads at N ≤ 512 FP32 by 0.5–8%, but from N = 1024 up 8 × 3 is
  ahead of both: 12 × 2 by 1.5–2% (12 × 2 is 0.3% ahead only at FP64
  N = 1024), 14 × 2 by 3–5%. With TileM = 512 their last strips are 8 rows
  (42 × 12 + 8, 36 × 14 + 8), a fast shape, so the remainder is not what
  holds them back; the sweep does not isolate what does.
- **Registers:** 8 × 3 uses 28 of the 32 ZMM registers, no spills. No
  shape in the sweep spills (14 × 2 uses 31).

With its default cache blocks ([blocks.md](blocks.md)) the 8 × 3 kernel
measures 327.9 GFLOP/s FP32 at N = 512 (91% of peak) and 294.7 at 4096;
FP64 162.0 at N = 256 (90%), 157.7 at 512 and 145.0 at 4096
([bench_zen5_defaults.csv](../experiments/block-sweeps/bench_zen5_defaults.csv)).

## Reproduce

```bash
cmake -B build -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release -DHPC_ENABLE_AVX512=ON
cmake --build build --target bench_gemm simd_microkernel
taskset -c 2 ./build/benchmarks/bench_gemm --benchmark_filter='^Avx512Tile' \
    --benchmark_repetitions=3 --benchmark_report_aggregates_only=true
taskset -c 2 ./build/benchmarks/simd_microkernel
```

A shape not in the sweep: `gemm_simd_blocked<Zmm, R, V, TileM, TileK, TileN>(A, B, C)`, or
a line `HPC_AVX512_TILE(R, V);` in `add_tiles()` of
[`bench_gemm.cpp`](../../benchmarks/bench_gemm.cpp).
